## What Mailchimp is for

Mailchimp is how a business stays in touch with the people who care about it. A
coffee roaster, a nonprofit, a software company: each one keeps a list of
customers, donors, or subscribers, and uses Mailchimp to send that list email
and SMS. Newsletters, product launches, order updates, a discount aimed at
people who haven't bought anything since spring.

The Marketing API is how you do that from code instead of from the Mailchimp
app. You might be automating your own marketing, running Mailchimp for clients,
or connecting a product your company sells to the Mailchimp accounts its users
already have.

## What people do with Mailchimp

**Collect people.** Contacts arrive from signup forms, a storefront, an import,
or an API call. They live in an
[audience](/marketing/concepts/audiences), which is Mailchimp's word for a
list of contacts.

**Organize them.** Sending to the whole audience works, and lots of people do
it. The results are usually better when the message matches who's receiving it,
which is what tags and segments are for. Contacts get labeled with
[tags](/marketing/concepts/audiences/contacts), and
[segments](/marketing/concepts/audiences/segments) pick out groups like everyone
who opened the last three emails or bought a specific product.

**Send something.** Mailchimp sends in two ways, and what separates them is
what decides that a message goes out.

- **Campaigns** are sent by a person. A campaign is one message to a chosen set
  of contacts, sent once or scheduled for later: a monthly newsletter, a Black
  Friday announcement.
- **Automations** are messages triggered by events. A welcome email when
  someone joins, a reminder when a shopper leaves items in a cart, a follow-up
  a week after a purchase. You set the trigger once, and Mailchimp sends every
  time it matches.

Automations are often the reason to reach for the API at all, because their
triggers depend on data you send in.

**See what happened.** Reports record opens, clicks, unsubscribes, and revenue
per campaign. Engagement is also something you can segment on, so who opened
the last message can decide who gets the next one.

Mailchimp integrates deeply with e-commerce data. Connecting a store adds a
second model alongside the audience: [stores, products, carts, and
orders](/marketing/concepts/e-commerce). Purchases and abandoned carts become
the events that trigger automations, orders let reports attribute revenue to
the campaign that earned it, and what someone bought shapes the product
recommendations inside the messages they get next.

## What the API is for

Most work with the Marketing API is some mix of four jobs.

**Sending data in.** Contacts, purchases, and activity from another system land
in an audience so that segments and automations have something to work with.

**Getting data back.** Campaign and engagement results come out of Mailchimp
and into a database, a dashboard, or a spreadsheet, where they can sit
alongside the rest of what you know about the same people.
[Webhooks](/marketing/api-concepts/webhooks) push changes to you as they
happen; reporting endpoints let you ask.

**Sending messages.** Campaigns can be built, filled with content, and sent
from code, so a message can be assembled out of live data and go out the moment
that data says it should. Automations are set up in Mailchimp and triggered by
events you report through the API.

**Automating the work around all of it.** Imports on a schedule, contacts
tagged by rules, a weekly campaign that builds itself from last week's numbers.
Work that used to need someone at a keyboard runs on its own, and keeps working
the same way whether the audience is 500 people or 500,000.

## What the Marketing API does not cover

The Marketing API covers campaigns and automations sent to an audience. It has
no endpoints for per-user application email like password resets, receipts, or
shipping confirmations. Those come from
[Mailchimp Transactional](/transactional), a separate product with its own API
and its own docs. If the message is triggered by one person doing one thing and
goes only to them, that's Transactional, not Marketing.
